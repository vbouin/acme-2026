— New 02 · Cost optimization

The pre-clinic.
Getting to the heart of it.

In a two-hour interview, a good half hour is spent on profiling, building rapport and questions with predictable answers. That is senior consultant time, and you pay for it. A first pass, run by a bot, wins it back.

{{video:preclinique}}
{{slider:preclinique}}
How it works
  • A first pass run by a bot, by voice or in writing, on profile, usage and context.
  • The voice reads only the guide, word for word. Anything the model composes goes on screen, where it stays open to review.
  • The screening sheet fills in with the quote behind each value; anything missing is asked again once, never hidden.
  • The researcher finds everything in a fieldwork tracker: one box per pre-interview, each session's profile, the milieu hypotheses with their signals.
  • The human interview picks up where the first pass leaves off, on what calls for listening.
What it changes
  • More fieldwork for the same budget, or the same fieldwork for less.
  • Denser interviews: the consultant comes in with the context already in hand.
  • Better-vetted recruitment: profile mismatches show up before the session.

What we don't claim: that a bot replaces a qualitative interview. It prepares the fieldwork; it doesn't run it. A calibration phase decides whether the set-up holds; if it fails, we don't use it.

Format 02

Semantic fields, read in the interviews themselves

{{slider:semantique}}

The words people use to talk about a car change from one part to another, from one vehicle to another, from one study to another. We pick out every qualifier in the full transcripts, tie it to the part of the vehicle it describes and to the guide question that prompted it, then group them into fields of meaning.

  • Each adjective tied to its part: the front, the interior, the trunk, the price. Through syntax when the sentence says so, through the question when the question does.
  • Fields and where they overlap: does what gets called “rugged” outside turn up inside too? Does the exterior's promise hold up in the interior?
  • The concept against the competitors in the room, field by field, and by whether the person buys or not.
  • Two interview types compared: a consumer clinic on a compact SUV, a professional exploratory study on a small van.

— Research & AI

Our software,
our servers.

Speed comes from tools we wrote and host ourselves. That is not a housekeeping detail: it decides where your quotes live.

{{video:serveurs}}
What we guarantee
  • In-house processing. The analysis software is our own; nothing is handed to a third party.
  • Secure servers, in Europe. Your corpora don't cross the Atlantic to be read.
  • No client corpus in a public model. Training is switched off, and we can prove it.
  • A written retention period, separate for the raw recording and for the anonymized corpus.
What we don't delegate
  • Interpretation. Connecting the signals, understanding the why: that is the job.
  • A critical eye. Checking, putting in context, discarding what the machine makes up.
  • The recommendation. A decision is defended in front of a committee, not a model.
  • Coding. What the automatic analysis proposes stays open to correction, and gets corrected.

AI speeds things up. People decide. The line is short because the rule is.
